# CEO Discovery Questions — MedFlex Role-Play (Marcus Reyes)

The key constraint: Marcus only knows executive-layer facts. Questions that go operational get deferred ("talk to Kim"). Goals with him specifically:

1. Get him to elaborate on numbers he already cited — that's where contradictions live
2. Understand the board's success criteria (shapes which workstreams matter most)
3. Diagnose the two failed AI projects (failure mode ≠ "AI doesn't work"; it's adoption signal)
4. Surface what he believes vs. what the data actually shows

---

## Questions Designed for a CEO (Marcus-Appropriate)

### On the numbers he cited — probe for how he knows them

These questions invite him to explain the *source* of his numbers, which is where contradictions surface.

**Q: "The 7% mismatch rate — how does that number get to you? Who reports it, and what exactly counts as a mismatch?"**
> He said it's "hospital-flagged dissatisfaction" — but later may mention a quality score. If he describes two different measurement mechanisms, that's the contradiction. Follow up: *"So is 7% what hospitals report, or what your quality score shows — or are those the same number?"*

**Q: "The 4.2-hour fill time — is that measured from when a request comes in, or from when a coordinator picks it up? And who tracks that?"**
> Gets at whether the metric is real-time system data or a rough coordinator estimate. Also surfaces whether there's a system of record or whether tracking is informal (Aaron question).

**Q: "Nurse availability — when a coordinator needs to know if a nurse is free tomorrow morning, where does that information come from?"**
> This is the trap question. If he says "the app," you've set up the contradiction when he later mentions Kim updating the schedule by hand. Don't challenge it yet — just note it.

**Q: "The 12% no-show rate — is that spread evenly across the nurse pool, or concentrated in a subset? Do you know?"**
> If he doesn't know, that's a data maturity signal. If he does know, probe who tracks it and how.

---

### On the two failed AI projects — diagnose, don't accept the label

**Q: "The recommendation engine nobody used — what were coordinators doing instead when it was available? What did you hear when you asked them why?"**
> "Nobody used it" is not a root cause. You need to know: did it produce bad recommendations, was it too slow, did it not fit the workflow, or did coordinators not trust it? Each implies a different failure mode and different risk for the next system.

**Q: "The chatbot that hospital staff rejected — rejected at what point? During rollout, or after they tried it?"**
> Early rejection = fit/adoption problem. Post-trial rejection = quality or trust problem. Very different implications.

**Q: "What did the post-mortems on those projects tell you about why they failed? Was there one, or was it more of a 'we moved on'?"**
> If there was no structured debrief, that's a risk flag for the current engagement. Also surfaces whether Marcus has a clear theory of failure or is pattern-matching on surface features ("it was a chatbot, chatbots don't work").

---

### On board pressure and success criteria — what's actually being measured

**Q: "When the board says 'significant growth in 24 months' — what does that mean in concrete terms? Is it shift volume, revenue, geography, or headcount ratio?"**
> The answer changes which workstreams matter. If it's shift volume, matching throughput is the constraint. If it's geography, compliance/credentialing across new states becomes critical. If it's headcount ratio, coordinator leverage is the core metric.

**Q: "If you hit 10x shift volume with the same coordinator count, what breaks first — and do you know that today, or is that a hypothesis?"**
> Forces him to surface where he believes the bottleneck is, not just where he wants it to be. "I think it's matching" vs. "I know it's matching" is a significant difference.

**Q: "What does the board expect you to demonstrate at the 12-month mark to show this is working?"**
> Gets at interim success criteria. If the answer is "fill time under 1 hour," that's a different architecture than "mismatch rate under 2%."

---

### On workstream prioritization — let him rank it

**Q: "Of these three problems — fill time, mismatch rate, and no-shows — if you could only fix one in the next 6 months, which one moves the needle most on your board conversation?"**
> Marcus will answer this. It reveals where he feels the most board exposure. High-value signal for workstream priority.

**Q: "Where do coordinators spend the most time — finding nurses, verifying credentials, or managing exceptions after placement?"**
> He may not know precisely — that's fine. His belief about it is still signal. If his answer differs from what Kim would say, that gap matters.

---

## Contradiction-Catching Protocol

When you hear something that conflicts with an earlier statement, the framing that works with a CEO like Marcus:

> *"Help me reconcile two things you said — earlier you mentioned [X], but now I'm hearing [Y]. Which one reflects the actual state of things?"*

Don't grandstand. Don't say "that's a contradiction." Just name the two things and ask him to resolve it. He'll either clarify, acknowledge the gap, or defer it to the engagement — all of which are useful signals.

## What NOT to Ask Marcus

- Anything about how coordinators use specific tools → Kim
- How credentials are verified in detail → Linda
- Whether systems can be integrated → Aaron
- Root causes of specific operational failures → operational debrief, not CEO

Asking him these wastes your 60 minutes and signals you didn't read the brief. The coach will note it.
